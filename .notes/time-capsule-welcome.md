# Time capsule welcome experience

## Core idea

The archive opens as an old wooden time capsule floating in quiet digital space.

The chest holds the warmth and mess of real memories. The surrounding space represents the archive itself: dark, vast, and digital. The contrast between the two should carry the experience.

Cold space outside. Warm history inside.

## Mood

The experience should feel nostalgic, cozy, and slightly mysterious. Think late-afternoon attic light meeting a restrained cyberspace aesthetic.

The nostalgia comes from worn wood, aged brass, faded photographs, paper fibers, handwritten notes, and small signs of use. The digital side comes from darkness, sparse stars, brief binary rain, and the sense that the archive extends far beyond the screen.

It should not feel like a pirate game or a loud cyberpunk interface.

## Journey into the archive

1. The visitor begins on the normal public website. The page feels familiar and clearly offers a way to open the archive.
2. When the visitor enters, the page darkens. The usual website navigation and structure recede.
3. Matrix-style binary rain flashes across the screen for a second or two. It signals that the archive is loading or being decoded.
4. The chest appears within the falling code. As it becomes clear, the binary rain fades away.
5. The chest opens against a quiet field of stars. Warm amber light and a little dust spill from inside.
6. Memories emerge one at a time. A photo may rise and tilt, a note may unfold, and a fact may appear on a small archival label.
7. After the first reveal, the archive controls settle into place. At this point, the visitor should understand that they have left the website and entered the application.

The visual sequence is:

`website -> code -> chest -> memory -> archive`

## Memory interaction

The opening experience should reveal around five memories before inviting the visitor into the full archive. They can continue drawing memories if they want.

The selection mixes:

- Photos with dates or short captions
- Quotes or chat fragments on paper notes
- Personal facts on small archive cards

Each memory gets its own moment. New items should not burst out all at once. Photos can float, notes can unfold, and previous items can drift into the distance like a loose constellation.

The random selection should avoid immediate repeats and mix the content types so it feels considered rather than shuffled.

## Art direction

### The chest

The chest should remain old and physical:

- Warm walnut with softened, scuffed edges
- Tarnished brass fittings and latch
- A dark leather or cloth lining
- Fine scratches, dust, and signs of age
- A warm glow from within when opened

Small digital details may connect it to the archive, such as faint engraved coordinates or particles forming around its edges. These should remain easy to miss. The chest should not become a futuristic container.

### Space

The space background should stay quiet enough for the memories to lead:

- Deep navy and charcoal instead of pure black
- Sparse stars at different depths
- Barely visible blue or violet haze
- Very slow movement
- No constant shooting stars, bright galaxies, or large lens flares

### Memory objects

A small set of reusable physical forms will hold the changing content:

- Instant-photo frames
- Aged prints with soft, faded edges
- Folded or curled paper notes
- Ticket stubs or date labels
- Small archival fact cards

The real photos, quotes, and facts provide the variation. The frames and paper objects keep the experience visually consistent.

### Color and light

The world outside the chest uses deep navy, charcoal, muted blue, and a little violet. The chest and memories use walnut brown, aged brass, cream paper, dusty rose, faded blue, muted sage, and amber light.

The warm and cool sides should remain distinct. That contrast makes the chest feel personal inside a large digital archive.

## Visual assets to create

- Treasure chest concept sheet
- Finished opening chest model
- Walnut, brass, leather, paper, and dust material references
- Layered star background
- Short binary-rain transition
- Chest materialization particles
- Warm interior glow and dust motes
- Photo, quote, and fact-card templates
- Dark archive interface direction
- Reduced-motion version of the entrance

## Asset creation approach

1. Collect six to ten references covering old photo albums, keepsake boxes, worn materials, attic light, restrained star fields, and early cyberspace imagery.
2. Create three or four chest concepts, then commit to one silhouette and material treatment.
3. Design the photo, quote, and fact objects around the chosen chest.
4. Apply the same warm color treatment, grain, faded edges, and subdued shadows across the memory objects.
5. Test the full sequence with a small set of real memories before expanding it.

Generated images can help explore the mood, chest concepts, material references, and memory-card designs. The final chest must be a real 3D asset so it can open and live naturally inside the scene.

## Copy direction

Keep the writing short and personal. Possible lines include:

- "Welcome back. Your time capsule is waiting."
- "A few memories, saved for later."
- "Open the archive."
- "One more?"
- "Step into the archive."

## Guardrails

- The binary rain disappears once the chest has arrived.
- Space never competes with the memory currently on screen.
- The opening stays slow enough to feel deliberate, but short enough that returning visitors will not resent it.
- Visitors can skip the entrance and enter the archive directly.
- A reduced-motion option preserves the same story without the cinematic movement.
- The chest remains nostalgic and tactile, not cyberpunk.

## Open decisions

- Whether first-time visitors get a designed opening sequence or a fully random one
- Whether returning visitors see a shorter entrance
- How many past memories remain visible around the chest
- Whether visitors can save or favorite a memory during the welcome sequence
- Whether the public website begins in light mode or already hints at the darker archive
