# The Intended Flow

The product is a single arc, and every page exists to move you through it.

**1. Arrival.** A landing page that explains what Goodbye Chat is and why it exists.

**2. Get your data.** A page that walks you through requesting your archive from Snapchat, including a direct link to `https://accounts.snapchat.com/v2/download-my-data`. Optionally, a way to be reminded a month later in case the export takes time.

**3. Upload.** You drop your archive into the browser. Nothing leaves your machine.

**4. First insights.** The dashboard shows the things you would expect first: number of snaps sent, when you registered, longest streak, top five friends. Familiar ground.

**5. The main course.** This is where the product earns its existence. Interactive, personal, surprising. Candidate experiences include a physics simulation of friend-blobs sized by message count, a chronological rapidfire animation of every text and image in your archive so you can feel the scale in a few seconds, or a timeline of your earliest saved chats. The exact tools will evolve, but the goal is constant: produce the moments of forgotten-memory recognition that make the whole project worthwhile.

**6. Explore.** A selection of sub-applications — community-driven analysis tools that let you see your data from different angles. Reminiscing is exploratory, so this section is too. Finding a tool you enjoy is part of the experience.

**7. Gallery.** Browse every image from Memories and from chats in one place. Toggle chats on and off. Filter by year. Select a year and get a summary, a timeline, a story.

**8. Export and leave.** Instructions for moving your data to Immich, Google Photos, or local storage. Plus a downloadable zip of your chats with an `index.html` so you have a local-only version of the chat functionality, forever, with no dependencies.

**9. Further reading.** How the social media industry profits off your data, and a rough calculator of what your data is worth to Snapchat.

The product has two endings on purpose. The emotional ending — *your memories are yours, you are free to leave* — speaks to the Eulogist and the Escapee. The intellectual ending — *here is what was being done to you* — speaks to the Curious, who may need that argument to act on anything at all.
